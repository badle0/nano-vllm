# PR 6 Design — The Forks

*Companion to `01–03` (theory, code map, math) and `05–06` (evidence & predictions, implementation plan). Line numbers refer to `dev` @ `317e6f0`. Verdicts are labeled **forced** (correctness or evidence leaves no choice) or **chosen** (trade-off; argue with me). Each fork ends with what the tests must pin.*

**The framing the evidence forces, stated once:** the feature is *"make step cost proportional to work, then chunk"* — graphs and mixing are two load-bearing halves of one feature. Graphs alone: 8 chunk-steps × 6 ms ≈ 48 ms stall, a wash against today's 51. Mixing alone: every mixed step costs 45 ms eager and everything regresses. Together: the 11.2× stall collapses to one ~6 ms mixed step. Neither ships without the other; the commit topology may separate them, the release may not.

## Fork 1 — Step representation: dissolve `is_prefill` into the varlen layout

**Options.** (a) Universal varlen for *everything*, deleting `prepare_decode` and `flash_attn_with_kvcache`. (b) Keep both representations and run two forward passes per mixed step. (c) Hybrid: pure-decode steps keep today's path bit-for-bit; any step containing prefill work uses the varlen layout, with decode tokens folded in as `seqlen_q = 1` segments.

**Forces.** (b) is dead on arrival — two passes means two weight streams, which un-does piggybacking; eliminate. (a) is aesthetically clean but risks the workhorse: the decode graph path (`run_model:202–217`) is proven, carries `bench.py`'s 8.5k tok/s, and the math file's own falsifier says a bench regression means the design failed. Re-routing pure decode through varlen would also change its numerics (different kernel: `flash_attn_varlen_func` vs `flash_attn_with_kvcache`) for zero benefit on steps that already replay in 5 ms. (c) preserves the proven path unconditionally and uses the general layout exactly where generality is needed — and `prepare_prefill:139–148` is *already* that general layout; a decode token is its degenerate case.

**The forced sub-decision inside (c), found by tracing TP serialization:** the unified prepare must branch **per sequence** on `seq.is_prefill` (sequence.py:33/86), not per step. A decode-mode sequence ships only `last_token` to TP workers (`__getstate__:86`), so `seq[start:end]` on a worker would silently return `[]` and misalign the batch. Therefore: prefill-mode segments read `token_ids[start:end]` with the slot-window math (151–161); decode-mode segments contribute `[last_token]`, position `len−1`, and the last-slot formula from `prepare_decode:181` — i.e. the unified prepare is a per-sequence merge of the two existing prepares, and TP serialization keeps working with zero changes. (TP=1, our scope, never pickles; this is future-correctness, and it's free.)

**Context and ordering.** `Context.is_prefill` becomes "this is a ragged/varlen step"; pure-decode steps set it False and nothing downstream changes. Batch order = the `seqs` list order the scheduler returns — temperatures, the sampler broadcast, and postprocess's `strict` zip all key off that list already, so alignment is inherited, not engineered. Logits gather (one row per segment, off `cu_seqlens_q`) stays *outside* any graph, same as decode's `compute_logits(outputs[:bs])` pattern.

**Verdict: (c), chosen — with the per-seq mode branch forced.** Pin with: pure-decode steps byte-identical pre/post refactor; a monolithic prefill routed through the unified prepare byte-identical to today's; a hand-built mixed layout whose decode rows match `flash_attn_with_kvcache` outputs at token level.

## Fork 2 — Scheduler policy: decode-first budget

**Options.** (a) Sarathi decode-first: admit every running sequence (1 token each, existing `can_append`/preempt logic verbatim), then spend the remaining budget on waiting sequences' chunks. (b) Prefill-first-but-mixed: chunks first, decodes fill the remainder.

**Forces.** The feature's entire purpose is the ITL bound, and only decode-first guarantees it: decodes never wait, and the worst gap any decoder sees is one step at budget. (b) can starve decodes whenever chunks fill τ and buys nothing in exchange. (a) also keeps the preemption machinery untouched — the decode-admission loop is today's `61–73` verbatim, run first.

**Multi-sequence chunking, resolved by an invariant rather than a knob:** iterate `waiting` FIFO after decode admission; give each sequence `min(remaining_work, remaining_budget)`; stop at zero budget. Consequences that fall out for free: at most one *partial* chunk per step (the last admitted sequence), and — since the mid-chunk head re-takes budget first next step — **at most one mid-chunk sequence system-wide at any time**. That invariant keeps teardown, prefix-cache hashing, and the emission predicate in the regime they were built for; assert it in tests. The line-43 first-seq gate is thereby *generalized*, not deleted: any sequence may chunk, but only the frontier one is ever partial.

**The emission predicate survives unchanged — with a proof sketch, not a hope.** For a decode-mode sequence, pre-append `num_cached_tokens == num_tokens` always (postprocess advances by the scheduled 1, landing exactly on the pre-append length), so `num_cached < num_tokens` is false and the gate at 92 passes it through; for a mid-chunk sequence it is true and the gate skips. The gate never needed to know what kind of *step* it was in — only what kind of *sequence*. This retro-validates centralizing it in `postprocess` (the streaming PR's bet) and resolves the old conjecture that the `is_prefill` conjunct is redundant: keep the flag in the zero-behavior commit for conservatism, delete it in a later cleanup with its own gate.

**Budget accounting:** decodes charge 1, chunks charge their size, total ≤ τ, `max_num_seqs` caps the list. The equal-τ ≠ equal-cost caveat (attention scales with prefix, not chunk) stays a documented refinement (cost-aware charging), not v1 scope — at 4k contexts on this model the attention term is ≤ ~30% of step FLOPs.

**Verdict: (a), chosen — decode-first, FIFO, ≤1 partial, generalized head-chunking.** Pin with: the system-wide ≤1-mid-chunk assert; decode admission under mixed load (no decoder ever skips a step while unpreempted); the existing `test_chunked_prefill_emission` re-run against the new scheduler; preempt-mid-mixed-step exercised.

## Fork 3 — Graph bucketing for varlen steps (the heart of the fix)

**What capture bakes vs what replay can rewrite** — the physics of the fork: baked are tensor *addresses* (hence persistent buffers), tensor *shapes*, and kernel *grid dimensions* (flash-varlen's grid derives from the host-side `max_seqlen_q` at call time). Rewritable are buffer *contents*: input_ids, positions, both `cu_seqlens`, slot_mapping (−1 sentinel already suppresses padded writes — attention.py:23, reused from decode padding at run_model:211), block_tables entries.

**The bucket key, and the probe that decides its rank.** A varlen graph is keyed by at most three numbers: padded token count `T_pad`, padded segment count `S_pad`, and the baked `max_seqlen_q` ceiling `M`. Two open questions from the probes govern how many axes survive: (i) if `flash_attn_varlen_func` tolerates **zero-length segments** (repeated values in `cu_seqlens_q`), then `S_pad` collapses to a single value (`max_num_seqs + 1` slots, unused segments empty) and the key is one-dimensional in `T_pad`; (ii) the cost of **oversizing `M`** (capture at `M = T_pad`, feed shorter real segments — oversized grid blocks should early-exit off the device-side cu_seqlens, but "should" is a probe, not a fact). Both are ten-line experiments listed in file 06; run them before freezing the scheme. Planning number either way: `T_pad ∈ {128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096}` gives 6–12 graphs against the existing 36 decode graphs — same order of startup cost (~0.1 s each) and pool memory, captured into the *same* `graph_pool` with the same largest-first discipline.

**Eager fallback, priced rather than feared:** any varlen step not matching a bucket runs eager at a measured 45 ms — `enforce_eager`, warmup, `T > 4096` monolithic prefills (which cost ~46 ms eager today anyway, so no regression), and any capture-unsupported corner. The design goal is steady-state bucket hit rate ≈ 100% for τ-bounded mixed steps; instrument the miss count so the claim is checkable.

**The rejected alternative, with receipts:** `torch.compile(mode="reduce-overhead")` promises the same dispatch elimination generically — and this project already holds a minimal reproduction of Inductor crashing on **exactly this workload shape** (dynamic leading dimensions, second distinct batch size, torch 2.10 CUDA — `repro_inductor.py`, re-confirmed this month). Mixed varlen steps are dynamic-shaped by construction. Manual capture is more code but is now *feasibility-proven on both attention branches* at 6.06/6.26 ms; compile is a lottery ticket against a compiler bug we've personally filed the shape of. Rejected for v1; revisit when the fence comes off.

**Verdict: manual CUDA-graph buckets, chosen; key rank decided by the two probes.** Pin with: graph-vs-eager **bitwise** logits equality per bucket (same kernels, same order — replay is deterministic, so this gate is exact, unlike Fork 5's other axis); replay-time regression bounds per bucket; a padded-step correctness test (garbage rows never gathered, padded slots never written); fallback-path counter assertions.

## Fork 4 — Accounting and the public surface

`num_tokens`'s sign convention (llm_engine:60) encodes the dichotomy this PR dissolves. `StepOutput` grows explicit `num_prefill_tokens` / `num_decode_tokens`; `generate`'s tqdm reads both (mixed steps update both rates — a display improvement, gated to be unchanged for pure steps). The public `step()` shim keeps its `(finished, num_tokens)` shape — `bench_latency.py:22,27` *ignores* the second element, so the shim returns the legacy signed value for pure steps and `+num_prefill_tokens` for mixed ones, documented as such. `is_finished`, `stream`, `generate`, and every event contract are untouched; the emission predicate's survival (Fork 2) is what makes streaming chunk-mixing-proof without edits.

**Verdict: forced in shape (step contract sacred), chosen in detail.** Pin with: `test_step_public_contract` unchanged; the pinned-ref `bench_latency.py` (metrics-artifacts `5b6f013`) running unmodified on the new tree — which is also the headline experiment.

## Fork 5 — The equivalence gate, now two-axis

Axis one, **graph vs eager at fixed composition: bitwise.** Replay executes the identical kernel sequence on identical data — exact equality is the correct expectation and a cheap, powerful gate (any mismatch = buffer-rewrite bug, caught instantly). Axis two, **chunked/mixed vs monolithic: token-level, not bitwise** — composition changes kernel tiling, low-order logit bits drift, greedy argmax may flip on near-ties. The gate: greedy (available on `dev` — the sampling merge retires the old `gate/stream-greedy` workaround) token-match with divergences adjudicated by the PR 3 fp64 tie methodology; expected shape "near-universal match, tie-class exceptions," each exception classified. Plus the seeded-equivalence and all streaming gates re-run with mixing forced via the τ monkeypatch. **Pin with:** the two-axis gate suite; divergence classification recorded in the pr6 doc, not just pass/fail.
